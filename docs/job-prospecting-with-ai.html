01 The idea

Tailoring every application by hand is slow. Faking it is fatal.

  • A
    The slow part is the boring part.Re-reading the job, reshaping your CV, drafting a letter, finding the right person on LinkedIn — an hour a role, every time.
  • B
    So the toolkit does it for you.It reads the posting, reshapes your CV to fit, writes the letters, and gets you interview-ready.
  • C
    But with one hard rule.Recruiters spot invented skills instantly. So every single claim must trace back to your real CV.
The founding rule
“If a claim isn't in your master CV — or your addendum — it doesn't appear in the output.”
— the principle every part of the toolkit is built to protect
The addendum is an optional file for things that are true but aren't on your CV — a long-ago project, a skill you left off. It's the one sanctioned way to add anything.

02 The big picture

One CV in. Many perfectly-fitted applications out.

You maintain a single “master CV.” For each job, the toolkit produces a version re-emphasised for that role — same true facts, arranged to speak to what the employer is actually asking for.

04 The honesty check on effort

It won't dress up a bad match.

Every requirement in the posting is sorted into one of three buckets. That's what makes the fit score honest — and what stops the letter from claiming a skill you don't have.

DirectYou've genuinely done this — and it's in your CV.
TransferableClose enough that your experience clearly carries over.
GapYou don't have it — named plainly, never hidden.

05 Two ways to reach out

Answering an ad — or knocking on a door.

Flow A · the offer

There's a job posting

You give it the link. It scores your fit and, if you're a match, builds the full pack aimed squarely at that advertised role.

Flow B · the cold approach

There's no posting — just a company you like

You give it a company name. It researches them, helps you pick a role to pitch, and writes a “here's what I could bring you” speculative application.

A subtle but important detail: who are you actually writing to?

Writing to a consultancy or a recruiter as if they were the end employer is a dead giveaway. So the toolkit classifies the organisation and reframes the whole letter to match:

End employer — you'd work here ESN / consultancy — placed on their clients Staffing agency — temporary missions Recruitment agency — represents you to employers
